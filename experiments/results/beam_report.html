Beam search does not fix it

Keeping three candidate answers alive cost 3.6× the requests and got exactly the same one question right. Two upstream bugs turned out to matter more.

Verdict: rejected

The criteria were set before the run: beam search wins only if washington appears where greedy produced garbage, and the short-answer control does not regress. Neither arm ever reached washington. Beam search also made the jupiter answer measurably worse.

Finding 1 — the decisive one

The identity field is teaching it to spell jev

Both arms answered largest planet with jev. Every character request carries about_you, which reads “Your name is Jev — spelled j, e, v.”

jupiter and jev share a first letter. At step 0 the model scored j=0.41 over p=0.25 — reasonable either way. At step 1 it took e=0.35 over u=0.15, and the answer was gone. The spelling is sitting right there in the state, on every single call.

Beam 3 made this worse rather than better: with four jev-prefixed branches all scoring well, it produced jev jev jev jev . — beam search faithfully amplifying a contaminated distribution.

greedy largest planet → jev pa . miss
beam 3 largest planet → jev jev jev jev . miss

Finding 2

Mean log probability makes beams stop early

Beams rank by logprob / steps1.0 — the mean. Stopping is cheap under that rule: a short answer made of confident characters beats a long one that must pay for every uncertain step it takes.

Both arms spelled capital  cleanly and confidently (l=0.83, then space=0.93). Then the distribution went flat. Greedy stumbled on for six more characters; beam 3 took STOP at 0.14 — the single highest option — and declared capital . finished. It spent 42 requests to produce a shorter wrong answer than greedy's 14.

greedy capital of united states → capital ca p . miss
beam 3 capital of united states → capital . miss

Finding 3 — against my own prediction

The trap this test was built for never happened

The case for beam search was the was → washington trap: a partial word that is itself a real word, where the space scored 0.87 against 0.05 for the letter that continues the real answer.

In this run neither arm got that far. Both wrote capital  and stalled on the flat step after it — c=0.21, STOP=0.16, a=0.12, n=0.08. The mechanism beam search was supposed to repair never arose, so this test does not actually falsify it. What it establishes is narrower and still decisive: on the questions that do fail today, beam search costs 3.6× and changes nothing.

What to do next

Fix the state before buying more search

  • Stop shipping the spelling. Send about_you only when the question is actually about the model, or drop the “spelled j, e, v” clause. Free, and it is the difference between jev and jupiter.
  • Re-test the trap directly. Use a question whose answer contains a word-prefix trap and that the loop can actually reach — the united-states question dies before it gets there.
  • If beams come back, tune the length penalty. At 1.0 they are biased toward stopping; below 1.0 they favour longer answers.

Beam search is not disproven — it was never reached by the failure it was built for. It is simply not the next thing to pay for.
